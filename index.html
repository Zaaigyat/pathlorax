PREPARE YOURSELF

Your starter guide to work.

Useful reminders for students who want to prepare for future employment, training, or responsible part-time opportunities.

📄

Make a simple resume

Include your contact information, education, relevant school projects, skills, achievements, and volunteer experience. Be honest about your experience.

  • Use clear headings.
  • Check spelling and grammar.
  • Tailor your resume to the opportunity.
  • Ask permission before sharing references' contact details.
🎤

Prepare for interviews

Practice explaining your strengths, interests, and what you can contribute. You can use school projects or group activities as examples.

  • Research the organization.
  • Practice answering common questions.
  • Be polite and honest.
  • Ask questions when something is unclear.
🔐

Stay safe online

Be careful when searching for work online. Scammers may pretend to be employers or ask for money and personal information.

  • Never pay someone just to get a job.
  • Verify the company and its official website.
  • Do not share passwords or verification codes.
  • Discuss suspicious offers with a trusted adult.
🌱

Build useful skills

Schoolwork, personal projects, and volunteering can help you practice skills that may be useful in future careers.

  • Practice communication and teamwork.
  • Learn digital tools relevant to your interests.
  • Keep examples of your projects.
  • Continue learning and asking for feedback.
If you are under 18, check local rules and age requirements before accepting paid work. Involve a trusted adult when evaluating unfamiliar employers or opportunities.

UNITED NATIONS SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS

SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

SDG 8 promotes sustained, inclusive, and sustainable economic growth, productive employment, and decent work for all.

🌍

Why does decent work matter?

Decent work means more than simply having a job. It includes fair opportunities, safe working conditions, respect for workers' rights, and the chance to earn a livelihood. Access to education, skills development, and reliable employment information can help people prepare for the world of work.
